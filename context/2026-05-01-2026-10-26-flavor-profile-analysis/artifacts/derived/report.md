# User Flavor Knowledge Map

**Cuisines & Cultural Inspirations**
- **Peruvian/Nikkei:** Frequent orders of artichoke empanadas, quinua bowls, Inca‑inspired drinks, maki acevichado and takoyaki show a love of Peru’s Nikkei fusion and seafood‑driven dishes.
- **Japanese & Pan‑Asian:** Takoyaki, nigiri, dumplings (xiao long bao), bulgogi cupbap and Korean‑influenced combos indicate comfort with Japanese, Chinese and Korean flavors.
- **Italian & Mediterranean:** Multiple orders of spaghetti cacio e pepe, gnocchi, pizza diavola/margherita and croissant sandwiches highlight Italian classics and rich dairy.
- **Mexican/Tex‑Mex:** Crispy chicken tacos, burritos and guacamole show an affinity for bold Tex‑Mex flavors and corn‑based dishes.
- **American comfort/fast food:** Burgers, chicken nuggets, breakfast sandwiches and fries reflect indulgent American comfort food.
- **Peruvian street food & bakery:** Pan con huevo, croissant de jamón y queso, alfajor‑style cookies and morcilla cocktail reveal a fondness for street‑style snacks and bakery items.

**Dominant Flavor Profiles**
- **Umami‑rich:** Frequent cheese (pecorino, parmesan, cheddar), meats (beef, chicken, seafood), soy‑based sauces and miso highlight a crave for deep savoury notes.
- **Mild–moderate heat:** Orders like pizza Inferno, burritos and acevichado rolls suggest appreciation for controlled chile heat (ají amarillo, rocoto, guajillo, chipotle).
- **Creamy & rich:** Hollandaise, cheese sauces, hot chocolate and yoghurt parfaits point to a taste for creamy textures and dairy richness.
- **Crispy & crunchy:** Takoyaki, chicken nuggets, fried tacos, empanadas, croissants and cookies show a strong preference for crunchy, crackly textures.
- **Sweet & chocolate‑forward:** Regular chocolate bars, cookies and milkshakes indicate a sweet tooth for deep cocoa flavours rather than light fruit desserts.
- **Balanced acidity:** Items like coleslaw, acevichado (ceviche‑style), and Mexican salsas reveal comfort with bright citrus and vinegar notes to cut richness.

**Textures**
- **Crunchy/fried:** Breaded chicken, takoyaki, crispy tacos, croissants, empanadas and churros demonstrate enjoyment of audible crunch.
- **Tender & chewy:** Pasta, gnocchi, dumplings and sushi highlight a love of tender or gently chewy textures.
- **Creamy & saucy:** Hollandaise, cheese sauces, yoghurt, puddings and parfaits showcase creamy elements.
- **Layered/stacked:** Burgers, burritos and sushi rolls suggest appreciation for layered constructions and bowls.

**Key Ingredients & Spices**
- **Proteins:** Eggs (poached/fried/scrambled), poultry (chicken, turkey), seafood (salmon, tuna, octopus), beef (burgers, lomo saltado), occasional pork/ham.
- **Cheese & dairy:** Pecorino, parmesan, mozzarella, cheddar, queso fresco, yoghurt; preference for rich, full‑fat flavours.
- **Vegetables & herbs:** Artichokes, avocados, onions, cabbage, mushrooms, spinach, aji amarillo, rocoto, cilantro, basil, oregano.
- **Grains & starches:** Bread (brioche, croissant), pasta, gnocchi, tortillas, rice, quinoa, oats; many refined in orders but to be shifted to whole‑grain forms in the plan.
- **Spices & aromatics:** Black pepper, garlic, ginger, cumin, sesame, soy, miso, chili powders (guajillo, chipotle), ají peppers; sweet spices like cinnamon and clove (chicha morada).

**Preferred Techniques**
- **Frying & crisping:** Deep‑frying or pan‑frying (nuggets, tacos, takoyaki) to achieve crunchy exteriors.
- **Baking & pastry:** Croissants, empanadas and pizza highlight a love of baking and laminated doughs.
- **Poaching & slow cooking:** Poached eggs (Benedict), ramen broths and braised meats show comfort with slow, moist techniques.
- **Stir‑frying & sautéing:** Lomo saltado and noodle dishes require quick, high‑heat stir‑frying.
- **Grilling & searing:** Burgers, chicken and seafood often involve searing or grilling to develop Maillard flavour.
- **Layering & rolling:** Burritos, sushi, dumplings and burgers show a penchant for layered preparations.

The user’s taste is indulgent and eclectic: they crave bold, savoury umami with occasional bursts of moderate heat; love crispy textures juxtaposed with creamy sauces; enjoy global mash‑ups (Peruvian‑Nikkei, Tex‑Mex, Italian classics) and are unafraid of rich dairy and decadent desserts. Vegetables are welcomed when integrated with strong flavours (artichoke empanadas, coleslaw), while purely raw or bland foods rarely appear. The palate balances richness with brightness (citrus, pickles) and values artisanal preparation over dietary restraint.

## Artichoke Benedict with Trout (Peruvian × Nikkei)

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 45 min **Total time:** 1 h 15 min

**Mise en place (≈30 min)**
- **Station A – English muffins:** Mix whole‑wheat dough (250 g whole‑wheat flour, 250 g bread flour, 7 g yeast, 10 g sugar, 300 ml warm milk, 30 g butter, 5 g salt) and let proof; shape into 8 muffins, rest 20 min.
- **Station B – Artichokes & trout:** Trim and steam 4 large artichoke hearts (400 g), then slice into quarters; season 200 g smoked trout fillet and flake into bite‑sized pieces.
- **Station C – Hollandaise:** Juice 1 lemon (30 ml), dice 50 g cold butter, measure 3 egg yolks and 50 ml aji‑amarillo purée; set up bain‑marie.
- **Station D – Poaching setup:** Crack 8 fresh eggs into individual ramekins; bring a wide shallow pan of water to a gentle simmer with 30 ml vinegar.
- **Station E – Garnishes:** Chop 30 g chives and 10 g cilantro; prepare aji amarillo hollandaise ingredients and warm plates.

**Ingredients**
- **For the muffins:** As per Station A above.
- **Protein & veg:** 4 steamed artichoke hearts (≈400 g), 200 g smoked trout fillet, 8 large eggs.
- **Hollandaise:** 3 egg yolks, 50 g cold unsalted butter cut into cubes, 30 ml lemon juice, 50 ml aji amarillo purée, 2 ml Dijon mustard, salt to taste.
- **Poaching liquor:** Water, 30 ml white vinegar.
- **Garnish:** Chopped chives, cilantro, freshly ground black pepper.

**Method**
1. **Cook muffins:** Preheat a heavy skillet over medium‑low heat. Roll the rested dough into 2 cm‑thick rounds using a ring cutter. Cook muffins dry (no fat) until golden on each side (≈5 min per side). Split and keep warm.
2. **Steam artichokes:** If not already steamed, cook trimmed hearts in salted boiling water until tender (≈10 min); drain and sear in a hot pan with a drizzle of olive oil until lightly caramelised.
3. **Prepare hollandaise:** In a bain‑marie over barely simmering water, whisk egg yolks with lemon juice and mustard until thick ribbons form. Slowly whisk in cold butter cubes one at a time until emulsified. Add aji amarillo purée; season with salt. Keep warm but not hot to avoid scrambling.
4. **Poach eggs:** Create a gentle vortex in the poaching liquor and slide in one egg at a time. Cook 3 min for runny yolks. Remove with a slotted spoon to a paper towel.
5. **Assemble:** Place a toasted muffin half on each plate. Top with seared artichoke quarters and flaked trout. Gently perch a poached egg on top and spoon over warm aji amarillo hollandaise. Sprinkle with chives, cilantro and black pepper.

**Why this works:** Whole‑wheat muffins provide nutty depth and fibre. Emulsifying butter into egg yolks over gentle heat yields a stable, velvety hollandaise; adding aji amarillo introduces fruity heat and vibrant colour without excess fat. Poaching eggs in acidulated water coagulates the whites quickly for neat spheres, while smoked trout delivers umami and omega‑3s.

**Plating & Pairing:** Serve two halves per person on warm plates. Garnish with micro‑greens for colour. Pair with a glass of sparkling brut nature or a chilled chicha morada spritzer (sparkling water with purple‑corn reduction and lime) for a non‑alcoholic option.

## Birria Ramen with Sous‑Vide Egg (Mexican × Japanese)

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 1 h **Total time:** 6 h (includes braising)

**Mise en place (≈40 min)**
- **Station A – Birria broth:** Toast 5 dried guajillo and 3 ancho chiles until fragrant; rehydrate in hot water. Sear 800 g lamb or beef chuck in a Dutch oven; gather aromatics (1 onion, 4 garlic cloves, 2 bay leaves, 5 g cumin seeds, 5 g coriander seeds).
- **Station B – Ramen tare:** Combine 60 ml tamari, 60 ml sake (optional), 30 ml mirin, 30 ml fish sauce and 30 ml rice vinegar.
- **Station C – Noodles & toppings:** Prepare 800 g fresh whole‑wheat ramen noodles; slice 200 g shiitake mushrooms, 100 g radishes, chop 30 g cilantro, slice 2 limes. Set up sous‑vide water bath at 63 °C for eggs.

**Ingredients**
- **Birria:** 800 g grass‑fed lamb or beef chuck cut into chunks, 5 dried guajillo chiles, 3 dried ancho chiles, 1 onion, 4 garlic cloves, 1 stick cinnamon, 2 bay leaves, 5 g each cumin and coriander seeds, 500 ml beef stock, 500 ml water.
- **Ramen:** 800 g fresh whole‑wheat ramen noodles, 4 eggs (for sous‑vide), 200 g shiitake mushrooms, 100 g radishes, 100 g bean sprouts, 30 g cilantro, lime wedges.
- **Tare:** 60 ml tamari, 60 ml sake (omit or use 30 ml extra tamari for non‑alcoholic), 30 ml mirin or apple‑cider vinegar, 30 ml fish sauce, 30 ml rice vinegar.
- **Garnish:** 20 g toasted sesame seeds, 2 spring onions sliced.

**Method**
1. **Make birria:** Toast chiles briefly in a dry pan, then soak in 250 ml boiling water until soft. Blend with soaking liquid, onion, garlic, spices and a pinch of salt to a smooth paste. Sear meat in a Dutch oven until browned; add chile purée, cinnamon stick, bay leaves, stock and water. Bring to a simmer, cover and braise in a 150 °C oven for 4–5 hours until meat is tender. Shred meat and return to broth; adjust salt.
2. **Prepare eggs:** Submerge eggs in a 63 °C sous‑vide bath for 45 min (for jammy yolks). Shock in ice water, then carefully crack and slide into bowls later.
3. **Cook noodles & toppings:** Sauté mushrooms in a drop of sesame oil until browned; season lightly. Blanch ramen noodles in boiling water for 1 min (fresh) or until al dente; rinse briefly to stop cooking.
4. **Assemble bowls:** Place 1 tbsp tare in each warmed bowl. Add noodles. Ladle over hot birria broth and shredded meat. Top with mushrooms, bean sprouts, sliced radish and cilantro. Gently add peeled sous‑vide egg. Finish with sesame seeds, spring onions and a squeeze of lime.

**Why this works:** Slow braising extracts collagen from chuck, producing a silky broth enriched with toasted chiles for deep, complex flavour. The tare concentrates umami (tamari, fish sauce) and balances acidity (vinegar) to season the broth just before serving. Sous‑vide eggs cooked at 63 °C achieve custardy yolks that meld into the soup, echoing the richness of traditional ramen.

**Plating & Pairing:** Serve in deep bowls with long-handled chopsticks and spoons. The broth should be glossy with a gentle chile sheen. Pair with a crisp Kölsch beer (limit to one glass) or a chilled hibiscus‑lime agua fresca.

## Brown Rice Risotto alla Parmigiana with Artichokes & Scallops (Italian)

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 50 min **Total time:** 1 h 10 min (includes soaking)

**Mise en place (≈25 min)**
- **Station A – Rice:** Soak 300 g short‑grain brown rice in water for at least 30 min to soften the bran; drain.
- **Station B – Stock:** Heat 1.5 L vegetable or fish stock; keep warm on a back burner.
- **Station C – Vegetables:** Mince 1 shallot, slice 200 g mushrooms, quarter 2 steamed artichoke hearts (200 g), grate 80 g Parmigiano‑Reggiano.
- **Station D – Scallops:** Pat dry 8 large sea scallops (320 g) and season with sea salt; bring a sauté pan to medium‑high heat.

**Ingredients**
- 300 g short‑grain brown rice (soaked), 1.5 L hot stock (vegetable/fish), 1 shallot, 2 tbsp (30 ml) olive oil, 200 g mixed mushrooms, 200 g cooked artichoke hearts, 100 ml dry white wine, 80 g Parmigiano‑Reggiano, 20 g unsalted butter, 8 sea scallops (≈320 g total), 5 g fresh thyme, salt & black pepper.

**Method**
1. **Build the soffritto:** In a heavy saucepan over medium heat, sweat minced shallot in olive oil until translucent. Add drained brown rice and stir 2 min to coat the grains; this toasting promotes even absorption.
2. **Deglaze:** Add white wine; cook until mostly evaporated. Ladle in hot stock (~200 ml at a time), stirring continuously. Maintain a gentle simmer and add more stock only when the previous addition is nearly absorbed. This process takes about 40 min; the rice should be creamy yet toothsome.
3. **Cook vegetables:** In a separate pan, sauté mushrooms in a drizzle of olive oil until browned; season with thyme, salt and pepper. Add artichoke quarters and warm through.
4. **Finish risotto:** When rice is just al dente, stir in sautéed mushrooms and artichokes. Remove from heat; vigorously beat in butter and grated Parmigiano‑Reggiano to create emulsified creaminess. Season to taste.
5. **Sear scallops:** Heat a non‑stick or stainless pan until very hot. Add 1 tbsp oil, then scallops. Sear without moving for 2 min until a deep golden crust forms; flip and cook 1 min more. The centres should remain slightly translucent.
6. **Serve:** Spoon risotto into warm shallow bowls; top with two scallops each. Drizzle with good olive oil and sprinkle with extra cheese and black pepper.

**Why this works:** Soaking brown rice softens the bran, reducing cooking time and yielding a creamy texture similar to arborio. Slowly adding hot stock and stirring agitates starch into the liquid, creating the classic risotto creaminess without cream. High‑heat searing ensures scallops develop a Maillard crust while staying juicy.

**Plating & Pairing:** Present risotto in a shallow bowl with scallops neatly perched. Garnish with micro‑herbs. Pair with a minerally Italian white wine (Verdicchio) or sparkling water with lemon zest.

## Chifa Lomo Saltado Quinoa Bowl (Peruvian × Chinese)

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 35 min **Total time:** 50 min

**Mise en place (≈20 min)**
- **Station A – Quinoa:** Rinse 240 g tricolour quinoa; place in a pot with 480 ml water and a pinch of salt; bring to boil then simmer 15 min until fluffy.
- **Station B – Beef & vegetables:** Slice 500 g beef sirloin into strips; marinate with 30 ml soy sauce, 5 ml sesame oil and 10 ml rice vinegar. Slice 1 red onion into petals, 2 tomatoes into wedges, julienne 1 aji amarillo, chop 20 g cilantro.
- **Station C – Sauce:** Mix 30 ml oyster sauce, 15 ml soy sauce, 15 ml Pisco (optional) or rice vinegar, 10 ml fish sauce and 5 g sugar (or 5 ml honey).
- **Station D – Fried egg & fries:** Cut 2 medium sweet potatoes into batons; toss with 15 ml olive oil, salt and smoked paprika. Preheat oven to 220 °C.

**Ingredients**

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 30 min **Total time:** 2 h (includes chilling)

**Mise en place (≈20 min)**
- **Station A – Chicha reduction:** Simmer 500 ml purple corn chicha (made by boiling purple corn, pineapple rind, cinnamon and clove) until reduced to 150 ml; strain.
- **Station B – Mousse:** Separate 4 eggs; chop 150 g 70 % dark chocolate; measure 50 g honey, 200 ml heavy cream.
- **Station C – Pineapple:** Peel and core ½ pineapple (300 g); cut into 2 cm cubes. Toss with 10 g coconut sugar and 5 ml lime juice.

**Ingredients**
- 150 g dark chocolate (70 %), 4 eggs separated, 50 g honey, 150 ml chicha morada reduction, 200 ml heavy cream, pinch of salt.
- Roasted pineapple: 300 g pineapple cubes, 10 g coconut sugar, 5 ml lime juice.

**Method**
1. **Make mousse base:** Melt dark chocolate in a heatproof bowl over a bain‑marie. Remove from heat; stir in honey and chicha reduction until smooth. Allow to cool slightly. Whisk in egg yolks one at a time.

3. **Fold:** Fold egg whites into the chocolate base in two additions, keeping as much air as possible. Gently fold in whipped cream. Divide among glasses and chill for at least 90 min.
4. **Roast pineapple:** Spread pineapple on a lined baking sheet; sprinkle with coconut sugar and lime juice. Roast at 200 °C for 15 min until caramelised. Cool.

- **Station B – Aioli:** Separate 1 egg yolk; measure 5 ml lemon juice, 5 ml white vinegar, 5 g Dijon mustard, 50 g aji amarillo paste and 100 ml olive oil. Mince 1 garlic clove.

**Ingredients**
- **Crisps:** 100 g Parmigiano‑Reggiano, 50 g aged cheddar, 2 g smoked paprika.
- **Aji aioli:** 1 egg yolk, 5 ml lemon juice, 5 ml white vinegar, 5 g Dijon mustard, 50 g aji amarillo paste, 100 ml olive oil, 1 minced garlic clove, salt.

**Method**
1. **Bake crisps:** Preheat oven to 200 °C. On a silicone‑lined sheet, spoon 15 g mounds of cheese mixture, leaving space to spread. Flatten gently and sprinkle with smoked paprika. Bake for 6–8 min until golden and lace‑like. Cool completely to crisp.
2. **Make aioli:** In a bowl, whisk egg yolk, lemon juice, vinegar and mustard. Slowly drizzle in olive oil while whisking until thick and emulsified. Stir in aji amarillo paste and minced garlic; season.
3. **Serve:** Plate crisps with a bowl of aioli for dipping.

**Why this works:** Parmesan’s low moisture content melts into lacy crisps that firm up upon cooling. Adding cheddar provides additional sharpness. Making aioli from scratch allows control over oil and eliminates additives; aji amarillo brings floral heat and golden hue.

**Plating & Pairing:** Serve as a pre‑meal snack with sparkling wine or a chilled lager.

## Smoked Salmon & Avocado Whole‑Grain Bagel (American × Peruvian)

- **Station B – Cure salmon:** Rub 400 g salmon fillet with 50 g salt, 50 g sugar and zest of 1 orange; cure 2 h, rinse, pat dry and cold‑smoke (or bake at 90 °C until just opaque if no smoker).

## Sous‑Vide Short Ribs with Purple Potato Purée & Sautéed Greens (American × Peruvian)

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 1 h **Total time:** 24 h (includes sous‑vide)

**Mise en place (≈35 min)**
- **Station A – Ribs:** Season 1.2 kg beef short ribs with 20 g salt, 10 g black pepper, 10 g brown sugar and 20 g miso paste. Vacuum‑seal with 2 crushed garlic cloves and 2 sprigs thyme. Preheat sous‑vide bath to 68 °C.
- **Station B – Potatoes:** Peel and cube 600 g purple potatoes; simmer in salted water until tender. Warm 100 ml whole milk and 50 g butter.
- **Station C – Greens:** Wash and chiffonade 300 g Swiss chard or kale. Slice 2 garlic cloves. Toast 30 g chopped pecans for garnish.

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg beef short ribs, 20 g salt, 10 g pepper, 10 g brown sugar, 20 g miso paste, 2 garlic cloves, 2 thyme sprigs.
- Purple potato purée: 600 g purple potatoes, 100 ml whole milk, 50 g butter, 20 g Parmesan, salt.
- Sautéed greens: 300 g Swiss chard or kale, 2 garlic cloves, 15 ml olive oil, 30 g pecans.
- Glaze: 60 ml pomegranate molasses, 30 ml red wine, 10 ml balsamic vinegar.

3. **Sauté greens:** Heat olive oil; sauté garlic until fragrant. Add chard and cook until wilted; season and finish with toasted pecans.
4. **Serve:** Spoon potato purée onto plates. Top with a rib portion, brush with glaze and arrange greens alongside.

**Why this works:** Long, low‑temperature cooking converts collagen into gelatin without drying the meat, yielding fork‑tender ribs. Miso and brown sugar in the cure enhance savoury depth and promote browning when seared. Purple potatoes provide antioxidants and a vivid colour contrast.

**Plating & Pairing:** Plate with glaze drizzled around and garnish with micro‑herbs. Pair with a bold Malbec or a tart pomegranate mocktail.

## Spaghetti Cacio e Pepe Oatmeal (Italian × Andean)

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 15 min **Total time:** 25 min

- **Station B – Mushrooms:** Clean and slice 200 g porcini or mixed mushrooms; mince 1 garlic clove; pick fresh thyme.

**Ingredients**

## Takoyaki‑Inspired Omelette Roll (Japanese)

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 20 min **Total time:** 35 min

**Mise en place (≈15 min)**
- **Station A – Batter:** Beat 6 eggs with 30 ml dashi (or vegetable stock), 5 ml soy sauce and 5 ml mirin. Dice 100 g cooked octopus tentacle into small cubes. Mix 30 g chopped scallions, 20 g pickled ginger and 20 g tenkasu (tempura bits or toasted breadcrumbs).
- **Station B – Sauces:** Mix 30 ml Worcestershire sauce, 30 ml ketchup, 10 ml soy sauce and 10 g honey for okonomiyaki‑style sauce. Prepare 50 g mayonnaise blended with 10 g yuzu juice or lime.
- **Station C – Toppings:** Shave 10 g bonito flakes (katsuobushi) and chop 10 g aonori (seaweed flakes).

**Ingredients**
- 6 eggs, 30 ml dashi, 5 ml soy sauce, 5 ml mirin, 100 g cooked octopus, 30 g scallions, 20 g pickled ginger, 20 g tenkasu, 30 ml Worcestershire sauce, 30 ml ketchup, 10 ml soy sauce, 10 g honey, 50 g mayonnaise, 10 g yuzu or lime juice, bonito flakes, aonori.

**Method**
1. **Cook omelette:** Heat a rectangular tamagoyaki pan or non‑stick skillet over medium heat. Lightly oil. Pour a thin layer of egg mixture to coat. Sprinkle with octopus, scallions, ginger and tenkasu. Once set, roll omelette toward you. Repeat layering and rolling until all egg is used, creating multiple layers.
2. **Glaze & slice:** Brush the rolled omelette with okonomiyaki sauce and drizzle with yuzu mayo. Sprinkle with bonito flakes and aonori; the flakes will undulate from residual heat.
3. **Serve:** Slice into rounds and serve warm.

**Why this works:** Tamagoyaki’s layered technique produces a tender yet structured omelette. Octopus pieces mimic takoyaki filling, while tenkasu adds crunch. The sweet‑savory sauce and mayo provide richness and tang, and bonito flakes contribute smoky umami.

**Plating & Pairing:** Arrange slices neatly and garnish with extra scallions. Pair with cold green tea or a light pilsner.

## Whole‑Grain Chilaquiles with Poached Eggs and Salsa Roja (Mexican)

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 30 min **Total time:** 50 min

**Mise en place (≈15 min)**
- **Station A – Tortillas:** Cut 8 whole‑grain corn tortillas into wedges; brush with 15 ml oil and bake at 200 °C for 10 min until crisp.
- **Station B – Salsa roja:** Roast 5 tomatoes, 2 cloves garlic, 1 onion and 2 dried guajillo chiles; blend with 10 ml lime juice, 5 g cumin and salt.
- **Station C – Garnish:** Crumble 100 g queso fresco, slice 1 avocado, chop 20 g cilantro. Prepare 4 eggs for poaching.

**Ingredients**
- 8 whole‑grain corn tortillas, 5 tomatoes, 2 garlic cloves, 1 onion, 2 dried guajillo chiles, 10 ml lime juice, 5 g cumin, salt, 100 g queso fresco, 1 avocado, 20 g cilantro, 4 eggs.

## Whole‑Grain Croissant with Jamón del País & Queso Fresco (French × Peruvian)

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 1 h **Total time:** 12 h (includes laminating/proofing)

**Mise en place (≈1 h)**
- **Station A – Dough:** Prepare détrempe: combine 200 g whole‑wheat flour, 200 g bread flour, 10 g yeast, 40 g sugar, 8 g salt, 220 ml cold milk and 50 g melted butter. Knead and refrigerate.
- **Station B – Butter block:** Flatten 250 g cold butter between parchment to a 20 cm square. Chill.
- **Station C – Filling:** Slice 200 g jamón del país (homemade or high‑quality ham free of nitrites) and 200 g queso fresco; prepare 50 g rocoto jam by simmering 100 g rocoto with 30 g honey and 10 ml vinegar until thick.

**Ingredients**
- 200 g whole‑wheat flour, 200 g bread flour, 10 g instant yeast, 40 g sugar, 8 g salt, 220 ml milk, 50 g butter, 250 g butter for laminating.
- Filling: 200 g jamón del país, 200 g queso fresco, 50 g rocoto jam.

**Method**
1. **Laminate dough:** Encapsulate butter block within détrempe and perform three single folds, resting dough in the refrigerator between folds to keep butter cold. Roll to 0.5 cm thick, cut into triangles and shape into croissants. Proof 2 h until puffy.
2. **Bake:** Brush with egg wash and bake at 200 °C for 15 min until deeply golden and flaky.
3. **Assemble:** Split croissants; spread rocoto jam. Fill with slices of jamón del país and queso fresco. Serve warm.

**Why this works:** Whole‑wheat flour is tempered with bread flour to achieve flakiness while increasing fibre. Maintaining cold butter layers during lamination creates steam when baked, yielding distinct layers. Rocoto jam offers sweet heat to balance rich ham and cheese.

**Plating & Pairing:** Serve with a side salad of arugula and orange segments. Pair with a cappuccino or sparkling orange juice.

## Whole‑Grain Gnocchi a la Huancaína with Grilled Prawns (Italian × Peruvian)

**Servings:** 4 **Active time:** 1 h **Total time:** 1 h 20 min

**Mise en place (≈30 min)**
- **Station A – Gnocchi:** Bake 600 g russet potatoes until fluffy; peel and rice while hot. Mix with 150 g whole‑wheat flour, 50 g grated Parmesan, 1 egg yolk and 5 g salt. Roll into ropes and cut into 2 cm pieces; press with a fork.
- **Station B – Huancaína sauce:** Blend 200 g queso fresco, 100 g aji amarillo paste, 200 ml evaporated milk, 2 crackers made from whole grains (for body), 2 garlic cloves and 30 ml olive oil until smooth.
- **Station C – Prawns:** Clean 12 large prawns (≈400 g); marinate with 10 ml olive oil, 5 ml lime juice, 1 g smoked paprika and salt.

**Ingredients**
- 600 g russet potatoes, 150 g whole‑wheat flour, 50 g Parmesan, 1 egg yolk, 5 g salt.
- Huancaína sauce: 200 g queso fresco, 100 g aji amarillo paste, 200 ml evaporated milk, 2 whole‑grain crackers, 2 garlic cloves, 30 ml olive oil.
- 12 large prawns, olive oil, lime juice, smoked paprika, salt.
- Garnish: Chopped olives, boiled quail eggs (optional), parsley.

**Method**
1. **Form gnocchi:** Combine potatoes with flour, Parmesan, yolk and salt until a soft dough forms. Avoid overworking to keep light. Roll into ropes and cut. Dust with flour.
2. **Cook gnocchi:** Boil salted water. Drop gnocchi in batches; cook until they float (≈2 min). Transfer to ice water briefly, then drain and toss with olive oil.
3. **Make sauce:** Blend all sauce ingredients until smooth; adjust thickness with milk. Warm gently in a saucepan; do not boil.
4. **Grill prawns:** Heat a grill pan; cook marinated prawns for 1 – 2 min per side until just opaque.
5. **Sauté gnocchi:** In a skillet, heat 20 ml olive oil; sauté gnocchi until lightly golden. Toss with huancaína sauce. Top with grilled prawns, olives and parsley.

**Why this works:** Baking potatoes instead of boiling keeps them dry, producing lighter gnocchi. Using whole‑wheat flour adds nuttiness and fibre without making the dumplings heavy. Huancaína sauce’s emulsified cheese and aji mixture coats each gnocchi, while grilling prawns adds smoky sweetness.

**Plating & Pairing:** Serve in shallow bowls with sauce pooling around. Pair with a crisp Sauvignon Blanc or maracuya (passion fruit) juice.

**Ingredients**

## Whiskey Pecan Dark Chocolate Tart (American)
